The daily loop

Three shifts. No data entry.

The Mortgage Intelligence Core runs on a clock, like a plant. Each shift has one owner, one contract and one result I can check.

  1. 9 PMCapture

    Today’s client conversations are written into Notes. Overdue follow-ups roll to tomorrow, so nothing quietly expires.

  2. 5 AMResearch and enrich

    Scouts work the clients due today and over the next 7 days: email history, employer from verified W-2s, metro, and public professional profiles.

  3. 7 AMDecide

    One email tells me who to contact, why, and includes a draft I review. Every draft waits for me; nothing is sent to a client without my approval.

Built like a plant

Every automation clocks in.

An automation is not “done” because the code merged. It is done when it has four things, and a run without proof shows up as missing.

  • A job file

    The one set of instructions it follows.

  • An SOP map

    What it is for, what I should see, and which file to change.

  • A registry row

    Its schedule and where its results land.

  • A receipt every run

    The 6 PM Daily Automation Update shows succeeded, needs help, failed or “No receipt.”

The scorecard target is simple: at least 80% of today’s contacts enriched by 7 AM. Risky changes, such as customer data writes or deployment controls, need an independent AI reviewer’s pass on the exact commit before they merge.

What we’re adding

The conveyor keeps growing.

Every gap in a record should become a next action. These are the stations being added, with their honest status.

  • Live
    Public profile research

    A bounded 5 AM lookup of public professional profiles, verified only when city and state or a W-2 employer match. Capped per day, never repeated within 30 days, sources and dates recorded.

  • Live
    Significant conversations

    Buying, refinancing, waiting, life changes and open questions pulled from email history, each linked to its source and kept apart from suggestions.

  • Designing
    Journeys

    Cadence by purpose. The renter journey runs today; post-closing is paused until next year; more journeys are being designed.

  • Evaluating
    Property enrichment scout

    Property and mortgage records from a provider such as ATTOM, so ownership, equity and rate gaps become timely conversations.

  • Evaluating
    Contact enrichment scout

    Verified phone and email from a provider such as Melissa, so a stale contact becomes reachable.

  • Planned
    Retargeting and outcomes

    Replies and closed deals decide who gets researched deeper and who sees my content next.

Paid data starts only when the monthly cost is known and approved. Spending is a decision I make, not one an agent makes.

Where we need to improve

The honest
punch list.

Not every station clocks in yet. Some newer parts, like the relationship factory and the birthday preview, still need their first published receipt, and the daily scorecard run is built but not yet scheduled.

Outside property and contact data is waiting on cost, so some records still carry known gaps. They stay visible as questions instead of being filled with guesses.

My own mistake to watch: calling work done before the output I actually see is right. Acceptance now means reading back the real result, not trusting a merged change.

For hiring teams and partners

What this says
about how I work.

  • I model the business first. Households, properties, loans and conversations are designed as relationships before any automation touches them.
  • I write the contract, then measure the result. Each workflow has an owner, a schedule, acceptance criteria and a receipt.
  • I run AI agents like a team. One manages, another independently reviews, and none accepts its own work.
  • I protect people and money. No client sends, spending or data writes outside an approved scope.
  • I learn in public, from mistakes. Errors are recorded as events with a cause and a fix, then folded into the next decision.

Why I built it

A loan file
is a database.

The people, the property, the loan and the conversation all belong together. I built Database 2.0 around those relationships.

Tools that cost too much, did too little and didn’t connect pushed me to build my own. The mistakes helped me see what the system needed next.

I design the relationships, define the work and accept the result. AI agents implement most of the code.
