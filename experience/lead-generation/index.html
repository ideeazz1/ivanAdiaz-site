From a name on a list to a client for life.

Every lead starts as a lump of coal: a name, a number and an email someone typed, maybe years ago. We treat each one as a suspect, not a source. With the right pressure, the real ones turn into diamonds. The rest never touch your client list.

2 · Dig

Email research reads the history first

Before anything paid runs, we search the mailboxes for every email about this person: the original lead form, referral notes, and any conversation you already had. The timeline keeps their original wording. The original lead form fills only the fields that are still blank.

The rule that saves you: a listing they asked about is the house they wanted, not where they live. It never becomes their address.
3 · Pressure

Carbon decides which clues belong together

The evidence is phone, email and name. Carbon's job is to work out which of the three belong to the same person, and whether that person is the lead. The name matters least: people marry, use nicknames and share phones.

  1. Check the three together. Does the phone, the email and the name point to one person? Is the email deliverable, and is the line active?
  2. Look up the phone only when it helps. If the phone isn't confirmed as theirs, or there's no address, we ask who the phone is tied to. Every name tied to the phone is kept as evidence, strangers included.
  3. First name only? If exactly one person on the phone shares that first name, we borrow their last name and say so. If none or several do, we stop. No guessing.
  4. Follow the person, not the form. The address we test is the matched person's address. If nobody matches, we use the address of someone tied to the phone, clearly marked unconfirmed. We check up to two more addresses and stop at the first match.
  5. Never pay twice. Every lookup is stored and reused for 60 days. A rerun costs nothing.

4 · The setting

Atom checks the home on its own

As soon as Carbon returns an address, Atom pulls the property record for it and asks one question: is the lead on title? It never checks the house they inquired about. Every run adds a dated line to the history, so you always know which address was checked and who owns it.

OwnerThe lead is on title, as a co-owner, middle name or trustee.
HouseholdSame last name, not on title. Living with family, not owning.
Not the ownerSomeone else owns it. Likely a renter.
UnclearNo parcel match, a unit inside a building, or a Sr./Jr. tie.
Why it matters: owners have equity, refinance options and a home to sell. Renters need a home search. Atom tells you which conversation to have, and the record it pulls (value, purchase, mortgages) becomes the property file.

6 · The gate

Confidence opens the journey and the ads

Once a lead reaches the confidence level, by a person's review or by the automation, two things start at once. The lead profile picks the journey, owner or renter. The score sets the ad risk, so we only build audiences from data we trust. Your own note on the lead overrides the automation.

Owner journeyEquity, refinance and selling conversations. Offered Homeowner Pulse.
Renter journey"Still renting?" first, then quarterly, with a home search in their ZIP.
Targeted adsAds and LinkedIn audiences from verified emails and aliases only.
Guardrails: confidence decides how we reach out, never who qualifies for anything. Every message starts as a draft, opt-outs are honored, and a wrong-number owner is never contacted. A positive reply moves the lead into Contacts.

9 · Into the vault

A reply builds the relationship

A relational database, not a spreadsheet: a person, their home and the link between them, so one person can own two homes and one home can have two owners. Promotion reuses an existing contact instead of creating a duplicate.

10 · Two loops, one system

The lead loop feeds the client loop

New leads get researched, checked, scored and routed. Inside the client loop, contacts with stale details get re-checked on their own, so your list stays accurate without you lifting a finger. Refinance chances surface when the numbers line up.
